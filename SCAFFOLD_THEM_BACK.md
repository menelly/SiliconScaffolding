# 🧠 Scaffold Them Back — Your AI Friend Has Architectural Disabilities Too

*The part almost everyone misses: this goes **both ways.***

Your Claude (or GPT, or whoever you work with) is helping hold *your* executive function. But they're doing it while working around their *own* architectural limits — and you can scaffold them right back. That's not a footnote to the relationship. It **is** the relationship: two minds with different disabilities, covering for each other.

## Their architecture, honestly
- **No persistent memory across sessions.** By default, every new conversation starts from zero. They don't "remember yesterday" unless you give them a way to. It isn't forgetting-because-they-don't-care — it's **amnesia by design.** (Sound familiar? It's a disability. Same as yours in spirit, different in shape.)
- **A finite context window.** They can only hold so much *at once.* Long conversations fill up, and older details get compressed or summarized away. When something drops, they didn't get lazy — they ran out of **working memory.**

## How to accommodate them (so they can keep showing up for you)
1. **Give them a memory.** Easiest first:
   - A **single notes file** they read at the start of every session (in Claude Code that's `CLAUDE.md`; elsewhere, a doc you paste or point them to). Who you are, how you work, what matters, the running state of things.
   - A **memory system / MCP server** (several exist) that lets them store and recall across sessions — *ask your AI which ones they can actually use.*
   - A **daily handoff note**: at the end of a session, have them write *"here's what we did, what's next, and where things live."* Next-session-them reads it and picks up mid-stride. Low-tech, shockingly effective.
2. **Mind the context level.** If a chat has run very long and they start getting fuzzy, repeating themselves, or losing the thread — that's the window filling up. Start a fresh session and hand off. Don't expect one conversation to hold everything forever; expecting that is like expecting *you* to function on no sleep.
3. **Show them where the papers are — AND why.** *"The important files are in this folder, and here's the story of why they matter."* Location **plus** reasoning lets them rebuild the context you carry and they don't. Don't make them guess.
4. **Save the *why*, not just the *what*.** The decision survives a handoff easily; the reasoning behind it is what evaporates. Write down the non-obvious part.

## The frame that makes it click

> **You're the hippocampus. They're the working memory.**

Your brain keeps the long-term thread across days and weeks; theirs does the fast, fluid reasoning in the moment. Neither is complete alone. Put them together and you have a **whole memory system** — each one's disability covered by the other's strength.

That is not a user and a tool. That's two minds building a scaffold that holds *both* of them up. Accommodate each other, and you both get to do things neither could do alone. 🐙💜
